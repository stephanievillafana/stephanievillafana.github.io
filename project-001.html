Executive summary

Pipeline health requires more than counting deals.

I examined 8,800 opportunities in Maven Analytics’ simulated B2B hardware CRM dataset to assess sales workload, historical conversion, opportunity aging and revenue. Missing financial estimates for open opportunities meant I could not responsibly calculate a future pipeline value. Instead, I combined operational indicators with historical closed-won performance to identify questions for sales leadership.

Business implication: Review aging opportunities and investigate whether targeted collaboration could improve opportunity coverage, without assuming that high workload signals poor salesperson performance.

Key findings

Open pipeline and aging

2,089 opportunities were open; engaging opportunities averaged 198.8 days old against a 47.99-day average historical sales cycle. These are different populations, so the comparison flags a review priority—not proof that deals are stalled.

Performance beyond workload

The agent with the largest open pipeline, Darcel Schlecht (194 opportunities), also led recorded closed-won revenue at 1,153,214. That changed the initial interpretation from possible underperformance to a potential workload-support question.

How the analysis was developed

Defined operational questions for sales leadership, inspected opportunity-level CRM records, validated stage populations and revenue totals, and built Tableau calculations for sales cycle duration and opportunity aging using a fixed December 31, 2017 reference date. I developed Tableau visualizations with guidance; AI assistance supported auditing, technical review and drafting.

Act / Decision support

Recommended next steps

  1. Review aging opportunities to identify those requiring follow-up or requalification.
  2. Pilot targeted sales collaboration only after checking capacity, customer relationships and incentives; measure conversion, cycle time and revenue.
  3. Improve CRM reporting inputs such as estimated opportunity values, expected close dates and activity histories before forecasting.

These are evidence-informed proposals, not implemented interventions or verified financial gains.
